## 1. Vegetation separates into pale groundcover, sharp sticks and granular crowns

The strongest local realism break is the very pale mint/cream groundcover below the right-hand trees, approximately x480–639/y176–201. It reads as light-colored bristles rather than foliage rooted within the darker forest. Above it, branch silhouettes are much more legible than coherent leaf masses: small dark/yellow flecks dominate crown interiors. These are two distinct material/sampling issues, not evidence for one global exposure correction.

**Groundcover mechanism.** `src/world/plants.ts:32` authors grass with `Color.setHSL(H=.225–.285,S=.33,L=.29–.37)` and stores those RGB values directly in blade vertex colors. Pinned Three defaults `setHSL` to the linear working space (`node_modules/three/src/math/Color.js:248`), whereas the fern hex color at `plants.ts:34` is decoded from sRGB (`Color.js:204`). Local CPU evaluation gives:

| Unshaded authored color | Linear luminance Y | Encoded sRGB swatch |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Grass domain minimum, H=.285/L=.29 | .342989856 | `#89a77a` |
| Grass midpoint, H=.255/L=.33 | .398633572 | `#9ab181` |
| Grass domain maximum, H=.225/L=.37 | .456297838 | `#abba88` |
| Fern hex color | .143256043 | `#4d7435` |

These are bounds of the authored HSL domain, not measured per-pixel colors or a reconstruction of the 36 sampled blade colors. Grass starts at 2.394–3.185 times fern luminance before their common blade shading. `plants.ts:13` multiplies vertex color by `.84+.16*u`; `src/render/materials.ts:16–22` adds height darkening and uses a white, roughness-.76, double-sided Standard material. This makes a local grass albedo comparison well motivated. It does **not** establish that the author intended HSL to be sRGB; blindly converting every procedural HSL, including multiplicative tree tints, is unjustified.

The material already receives scene shadows and cloud lighting. `src/main.ts:59` wraps its Standard material; `src/render/sky-lighting.ts:25–32` uses the wind-displaced, instanced world position for cloud attenuation. Three's `meshphysical.glsl.js:40–51` and `worldpos_vertex.glsl.js:4–18` likewise derive receiving shadow positions from the displaced vertex. Casting wind materials obtain the same displacement through `createGroundWindDepth` (`materials.ts:25–27`). Grass and ferns do not cast, so they lack mutual cast shadows, but a missing lighting hook is not the cause and a global casting change is not recommended. Wind changes positions without a matching normal deformation; that is a separate approximation, not proven responsible for this pale band.

**Crown mechanism and status.** `src/render/vegetation-material.ts:163–178,239` preserves fractional minified leaf coverage with alpha hashing on geometric LODs; LOD transitions add a static pixel threshold at lines193–201. The high-quality ranges are 24–48,80–135,245–325 m (`src/world/lod.ts:3`). At 640×360, subpixel leaf/branch coverage is a plausible contributor to the visible stipple; one frame cannot establish temporal shimmer or identify every crown's LOD. Far blending/coverage and linear output are OFF in this film. The integrated Island direct-response study targets base Island **far** crowns only and preserves alpha, near geometry and other forms. It therefore does not solve all foreground grain by construction. The separate proxy-shadow study is also not a cure for grass. Existing 267 shrubs are present; coastal sampling is OFF and remains an unaccepted placement study, not a grass-albedo fix. The genuinely missing calibration is the local pale grass response; near/medium crown sampling remains an independent open issue.

## 2. The cliff has texture frequency but insufficient readable rock mass

The upper/right exposed face is an immense cream/tan sheet with small fracture/noise marks. Some ribs cast useful shadows at its toe, but the large lit face lacks a convincing sequence of broad returns, recesses and smaller fracture planes. Increasing fine texture contrast would make the sheet busier without supplying that depth.

The existing rock albedo, normal and ARM use the same 5.7483 m source tile (`src/render/ground-materials.ts:44–71,133,146,169,193`). Three randomized phases reduce repetition while retaining that scale. The source-normal slope correction and accepted bedding alignment already exist; this is not evidence of an arbitrary wrong UV scale. `bedrockAlbedo` at lines27–37 reduces chroma on elevated inclined faces and caps mineral albedo at .65, so restrained mineral color is partly intentional. Geometry in `src/world/math.ts:65–103` combines smooth ridge/channel envelopes and noise fractures; the shore-distance envelope at lines119–123 also makes a broad smooth coastal rise. The frame exposes a remaining gap between scan-scale detail and convincing large geometry.

**Status:** the extra 23 m mineral-normal layer was visually rejected and remains zero; bedding alignment is already accepted and ON. Both principal-face and broad-recess cuts were rejected, not waiting for acceptance. The rounded east spur is OFF and awaiting its full-scene comparison; its local crops do not prove this wall repaired. The weathering study is a restricted sheltered-face albedo effect, and prior CPU sampling showed negligible support on the principal pale face. It must not be described as a solution to broad cliff mass. Preserve the source texture scale while judging the pending geometry candidate against this specific deficiency.

## 3. The beach reads as a continuous gray ribbon

The right-hand contact zone is an unusually smooth gray band, approximately x345–639/y183–212, with little distinction between reflective swash and damp granular sand. Its almost straight projected edge and fairly uniform width amplify the road-like reading. A naturally smooth sheltered shore is possible; the artificial impression here comes from shape, persistent material response and low resolved variation together.

`src/world/math.ts:14–15` shares one analytic shore curve across terrain and materials. The beach profile remains `.045*d` plus at most .12 m of noise through 25 m inland (`math.ts:105,119`). The film therefore has small geometric beach relief and a strongly shared shoreline organization. In the material, albedo grain is deliberately averaged away above a .18 m/pixel footprint (`ground-materials.ts:156–162`), leaving the authored pale low-chroma substrate. The same 18 s wetness history used for damp darkening (`src/world/coastal.ts:8,14`) drives roughness toward .20 (`ground-materials.ts:180–189`). A full wetting event still contributes approximately .792 after one 4.19 s runup cycle. This plausibly maintains a broad sky-reflective band after the water sheet retreats; it is not proof that every gray pixel is reflection.

**Status:** ripple amplitude .2 is already in this film and was accepted only as a narrow reduction of artificial rails. Chroma, shorter-lived surface film and ripple footprint filtering are default-OFF studies, not accepted improvements. Chroma changes almost no linear luminance; film drying changes gloss only; the derivative filter addresses unresolved normal frequency. None changes the underlying shore curve, beach width or geometry. Evaluate these existing separate controls first, while recognizing that the straight/uniform geometric organization remains unresolved if the material pair still reads as a ribbon. A motion pair is necessary for film drainage and ripple aliasing; this still frame cannot validate either.
