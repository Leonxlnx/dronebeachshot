## Baseline analysis

The historical `artifacts/continuation/final-diagnostics/mountain-wide-mode-0-960.png`
shows broad pale cliff surfaces with weak medium-scale mineral variation, a
narrow dark beach, and noisy distant canopy. It is a historical comparison;
the new production-browser baseline must be inspected before source changes.

Source findings:

- Sand grain is normalized by 0.142, but the decoded source texture's mean
  linear luminance is approximately 0.123. Resolved sand therefore darkens
  relative to the deliberately averaged distant sand. Correct the normalization,
  retain the derivative-based minification filter and preserve actual grain.
- Sediment classification uses only signed horizontal distance. A bounded
  source-terrain probe found partially sandy rising terrain up to 13.79 m.
  Slope/elevation support should remove sand from steep coastal toes while
  preserving the flat beach, sandy dunes and submerged sediment.
- The dry-rock helper removes 72 percent of the scan's chroma. Mineral
  calibration should preserve warm source variation and introduce restrained
  geological weathering at world scale. It must not paint fake lighting.
- The texture named `moss` is the licensed forest-leaf scan, not green moss.
  Treat it as litter on soil-supporting surfaces; do not apply leaf cover to
  vertical mineral faces or tint it green to simulate missing vegetation.

Inspected the actual production frames:

- `artifacts/refinement-2026-09-30/candidate-01-fast/flight-10.500.png`, 384×216.

Both show broad beige wall surfaces, tree crowns and trunks against exposed
light substrate, and a dark narrow beach. Candidate 1 does not resolve the
dominant land-material weakness. The differing resolutions and concurrent
integration changes prevent a pixel-paired or isolated color judgment. The
coordinator separately diagnosed the white sky as visible-canvas alpha export;
no compensating land grade is applied for that graphics defect.

With coordinator approval, Candidate 2 adds only soil retention on supported
planted shoulders. The shared habitat's soil channel and actual crown-coverage
channel modulate the existing rock blend. This uses the existing real soil,
leaf and mineral scans, not a green substitute for missing vegetation:

```
soilSupport = smoothstep(.04, .48, habitat.b)
            * smoothstep(.43, .76, normal.y)
canopyShelter = smoothstep(.08, .75, habitat.a)
retainedSoil = soilSupport * (.25 + .75 * canopyShelter)
rockBlend *= 1 - .72 * retainedSoil * (1 - sediment)
```

Bare soil-supporting shoulders can receive only the 25-percent base share of
this attenuation; actual canopy controls the remaining 75 percent. Soil-free
surfaces and fully sandy surfaces remain unchanged. The normal gate is exactly
zero at 65-degree slopes and steeper. Fully supported forest ground retains
at least 28 percent of its previous rock weight. Albedo, normal detail and ARM
use the same revised material mask. No new texture fetches, geometry, color
filter, wetness or lighting changes are introduced by Candidate 2.

A bounded numeric sweep checked 43,320 combinations of normal, soil, canopy
and sediment, including 12,120 steep-face combinations: no out-of-range weights,
no changed faces at or above 65 degrees, no changed soil-free or fully sandy
surfaces. TypeScript and diff checks pass. These checks establish the mask's
bounds only. Actual Candidate 2 image acceptance remains pending.

## Independent material read before the corrected normal A/B

Reinspecting `corrected-10_5.png` and the actual
`marble_cliff_03_diff_2k.webp` shows that the source is not featureless beige:
it has strong horizontal beds, cross-fractures and restrained gray/ochre mineral
variation. The rendered upper face loses that organization and reads as a few
large pale convex masses with fine grain. Simply darkening the material would
not create the missing intermediate planes or cast-shadow structure.

The existing 5.7483 m phased triplanar mapping deliberately interrupts repeated
tiles, but also prevents source cracks from connecting across phases. Its YZ
projection maps texture U to world height while the XY projection maps texture
V to height, so a horizontal bed in the source does not share the same world
orientation on both vertical projections. That source-level observation is a
possible reason for indistinct bedding, not a measured isolated visual cause.

In the upcoming 0/.12 comparison, look for actual readable intermediate planes
on the exposed cliff, not merely more speckle. The 23 m normal layer uses the
same scan at four times the albedo scale: if it produces broad dents that do
not agree with the colored joints, or only adds shimmer, reject the added cost.
It cannot change silhouette or cast new self-shadows. Leave material parameters
unchanged until the matched real images resolve this question.
