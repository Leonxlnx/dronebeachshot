# Opening camera composition

The early flight previously aimed beyond the falling hillside, filling most of its frame with open water. The camera now follows the descent with a smooth pitch correction between 0.25 and 9.5 seconds. Foreground crowns and the beach crescent become visible at 3 and 4.5 seconds, and the waterline reads more clearly at 7 seconds.

Position, bank and the 42-degree lens remain unchanged throughout the flight. The entire camera pose after 9.5 seconds and the slowing final glide are preserved exactly. The entry and exit use quintic blends with continuous first and second derivatives. Independent dense sampling measures a maximum angular rate of 56.41 degrees per second, below the existing 60-degree bound.

The four controlled before/after pairs use identical source, time, position and rendering settings. They are native development diagnostics, not browser acceptance. A first view at 1.5 seconds loses the horizon earlier; the sparse canopy and exposed pale slopes remain visible defects. This change does not establish the original sustained close treetop pass or final cinematography approval.

Two additional camera tests bind all route samples and later full poses to the pre-edit source and check the new angular blend boundaries. The suite has 52 passing tests, and the production build and asset/world checks pass. The native review harness also accepts the actual camera bank and an explicit vegetation tier; its default remains high. The vegetation setting does not change the diagnostic sun shadow size of 2048.

A separate bounded terrain sky-lighting experiment was rejected: its light-budget tests passed, but complete-scene comparisons showed negligible visual benefit. Its production changes were removed. The large artificial outcrop shapes remain a geometry issue requiring separate work.

The original art, actual browser cycles, final gallery, film and active-work requirements remain open.
